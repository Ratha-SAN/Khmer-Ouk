Many chess games would go on for ever if a lone Sdach could be chased around the board without end. Ouk Chatrang solves this with counting rules: the weaker side may start a count, and if the stronger side has not delivered checkmate before the count reaches a limit, the game is drawn. The rules are one of the most distinctive features of the game and the least familiar to players coming from international chess. This page explains them as the game implements them.

ហេតុអ្វីមានការរាប់Why there is a count

Pieces in Ouk Chatrang move only a short way, so forcing checkmate takes technique. The counting rules say that a side with a large advantage must prove it: win within the limit, or draw. The weaker side is not required to count, and counting is a choice. In the game, a "Start count" button appears for a side that is allowed to count, and the optional "Auto-count" setting starts the count automatically as soon as a side qualifies. The computer opponent always decides for itself.

កិត្តិយសក្ដារBoard's Honor

A side may start Board's Honor when it has three pieces or fewer and fewer pieces than its opponent. The count starts at 1, the limit is 64, and the count goes up by one with each move made by the side that is counting. If the count reaches 64 before checkmate, the game is a draw. The player who is counting may pause and start again at their own discretion; a restart begins from 1 again, so the limit applies to each run of the count, not to the whole game.

កិត្តិយសកូនអុកPiece's Honor

Piece's Honor applies to the very end of a game. It may be started only when no unpromoted Trey remain on the board and the player counting has only the bare Sdach left. The count does not start at 1. It starts at the total number of pieces on the board, both sides and both Sdach included. The limit is fixed the moment counting starts and never changes afterwards. Each move by the counting side adds one to the count. If any piece is captured, by either side, the count restarts from the new total number of pieces on the board, but the limit stays what it was. If the count reaches the limit before checkmate, the game is a draw.

ឧទាហរណ៍Worked examples

Two Tuuk against a bare Sdach

White has a Sdach and two Tuuk, and Black has only a Sdach. That is four pieces on the board, so Black starts Piece's Honor with the count at 4, and because White has two Tuuk the limit is 8. Each Black move adds one, so Black needs to make four more moves, and if White has not mated by then, the game is a draw. Four moves is enough to build the ladder mate, but only for a player who knows it, which is why the mating patterns are worth learning.

One Tuuk against a bare Sdach

With only one Tuuk the total is three pieces, so the count starts at 3, and the limit is 16. That gives the stronger side thirteen of the weaker side's moves, which is more room, but a lone Tuuk cannot mate without the help of its own Sdach, so a careless player can still run out of count.

A capture restarts the count

Suppose White had two Tuuk (limit 8, count 4), and a Tuuk is captured. The count is reset to the new total of three pieces, and the limit stays at 8, not 16. The count therefore goes up from 3 again. This is why trading off a piece in a counted ending is usually a mistake for the stronger side.

Board's Honor in practice

Black has a Sdach and a Trey (two pieces) against White's Sdach, Tuuk and Ses (three or more). Black has fewer pieces than White and has three pieces or fewer, so Black may start Board's Honor: the count begins at 1 with a limit of 64.

ភាពខុសគ្នាVariations between sources

Written sources, and players in Cambodia and Thailand, do not agree on every detail of the counting rules. The table of limits above is the commonly cited one, and the exact values differ slightly between sources. The game keeps the whole table in one clearly marked place in its code, so it can be adjusted if a better source is found. If you know of a variation that your family or club uses, or you spot an error on this page, please write to us.

គន្លឹះPractical advice

  • If you are ahead, aim for a mate you know. Two Tuuk or a Tuuk and a close Sdach (see the checkmate patterns) take only a few moves.
  • Before you trade a piece in an ending, check whether the capture resets the count to your disadvantage.
  • If you are behind, trading down to three pieces or fewer gives you access to Board's Honor, and a bare Sdach with no opposing Trey left gives you Piece's Honor.
  • Watch the count, which the game shows on screen.
