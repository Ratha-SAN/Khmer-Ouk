Most checkmates in Ouk Chatrang come from a handful of patterns, and almost all of them rest on one idea: because the pieces are short-range, you win by covering every square next to the enemy Sdach while something gives check. This page walks through eight patterns. Each diagram is a real position from the game's puzzles, and the game engine has confirmed that the marked move is checkmate.

របៀបរកអុកងាប់How to look for checkmate

Go through the same four questions every time. First, list every move that gives check. Second, for the Sdach that would be attacked, count the squares around it where it could go. Third, ask whether the checking piece can be captured, by the Sdach or by anything else. Fourth, ask whether the check can be blocked. If the answer to the second, third and fourth questions is "no", it is checkmate. One point catches many beginners: a Sdach cannot escape by stepping along the line of a Tuuk that is checking it, because that square is still on the line.

អុកងាប់ក្នុងប្រអប់1. The box mate (Sdach and Tuuk)

The Black Sdach is in the corner on h8. White's own Sdach stands on g6, where it controls g7 and h7 for the Tuuk. The only thing left is the back rank: the Tuuk goes to a8 and now controls g8 as well as giving check along the eighth rank. The corner has three squares and all three are covered, so it is mate. This is the most important basic ending, and the lesson is that your Sdach is an attacking piece: bring it close.

ជណ្ដើរទូកពីរ2. The ladder (two Tuuk)

Two Tuuk can mate without help from the Sdach. A Tuuk on h7 seals the whole seventh rank, so the Black Sdach on a8 cannot come down to a7 or b7. The second Tuuk lifts from g1 to g8 and checks along the eighth rank, and b8 is covered by the same Tuuk. There is nowhere to go. In a longer game you build the ladder rank by rank: one Tuuk shuts off a rank, the other shuts off the next, and the Sdach is pushed to the edge.

អុកងាប់ជួរក្រោយ3. The back-rank mate

Here the Black Sdach has hemmed itself in with its own Trey on d7, e7 and f7. The White Tuuk slides from a1 to a8 and gives check along the eighth rank. The Sdach cannot step forward because its own Trey are in the way, it cannot capture the Tuuk, and moving to d8 or f8 would leave it on the same line, still in check. Whenever your opponent keeps a row of pieces in front of the Sdach, look for a Tuuk that can reach the back rank. The defence is to leave the Sdach a square to breathe.

អុកងាប់ដោយសេះ4. The smothered mate (the Ses)

A Sdach surrounded by its own pieces is helpless against a single check that cannot be answered. The Black Sdach on h8 is boxed in by its Trey on g7 and h7 and its Tuuk on g8. White's Ses jumps from e5 to f7, giving check. None of Black's pieces can capture a Ses on f7, and the Sdach has no empty square to run to. Only a Ses can do this, because it attacks from a square that no neighbouring piece defends. The moral for defenders: do not pile your own pieces around the Sdach.

អុកងាប់ដោយគោល5. Mate with the Koul

With the Sdach on g6 supporting it, a lone Koul can mate in the corner. The Koul steps to g7 (one square diagonally) or to h7 (one square straight forward). Either way it attacks the Sdach on h8, and White's Sdach guards the square the Koul landed on, so the Black Sdach cannot capture it. Both versions of the move are mate. A Koul is a modest piece, but the combination of a Koul and a close Sdach is worth more than the sum of the two.

អុកងាប់ដោយនាង6. The quiet Neang check

The Neang is the weakest piece, but it can deliver mate precisely because it is cheap. The Black Sdach on a8 is already blocked by its own Trey on a7. White's Neang steps one square diagonally from c6 to b7, giving check. The Black Sdach cannot take it, because White's Sdach on c8 guards b7. And b8 is covered by the same White Sdach, so the corner is sealed.

អុកងាប់ដោយបើកផ្លូវ7. Discovered mate

Sometimes the piece that gives check is not the piece that moves. White's Tuuk on h1 is blocked by its own Ses on h7. When the Ses jumps away to f6, the h-file opens and the Tuuk gives check to the Sdach on h8. The Ses has not left the scene for nothing: from f6 it covers g8, and White's Sdach on g6 covers g7, so every escape square is taken. Two things happen with one move, and Black can answer neither.

អុកងាប់ដោយគោលស៊ី8. Take and mate (the Koul captures)

A capture can itself be the mating move. The Black Ses on g7 seems safe because the Black Sdach on h8 stands next to it. But White's Koul captures it from f6, landing on g7 with check. White's Sdach on g6 protects the Koul, so the Black Sdach cannot take it back, and the Koul also covers g8 by stepping forward. The lesson: do not rely on a recapture by the Sdach without checking whether the capturing piece is defended and whether the capture is itself check.
