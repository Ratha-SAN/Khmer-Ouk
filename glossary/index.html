A short vocabulary for players who know Khmer, English or both. The Khmer terms are the ones used in the game itself, and the English column gives the closest term from international chess. Where this glossary says "no direct equivalent", the idea belongs to Ouk Chatrang only.

កូនអុកThe pieces

KhmerWritten in Latin lettersClosest English termHow it moves
ស្ដេចSdachKingOne square in any direction. It may never step onto an attacked square.
នាងNeangQueenOne square diagonally. The weakest of the major pieces.
គោលKoulBishopOne square diagonally, or one square straight forward; never straight back.
សេះSesKnightAn L-shaped jump, like the chess knight; it jumps over other pieces. The Khmer word means "horse".
ទូកTuukRookAny distance along a rank or file, but not over other pieces. The Khmer word means "boat". The strongest piece.
ត្រីTreyPawnOne square forward; captures one square diagonally forward. The Khmer word means "fish".

ស្ថានភាពល្បែងStates of the game

KhmerEnglishMeaning
អុក / ជាប់អុកcheck / in checkThe Sdach is attacked and must be defended at once.
អុកងាប់checkmateThe Sdach is in check and cannot escape, capture the attacker or block. The side that delivers it wins.
ស្ទួនស្មើ / អាប់stalemateThe side to move has no legal move and is not in check. The game is a draw.
ស្មើdrawNeither side wins.
ការឡើងនាងpromotionA Trey that reaches the sixth rank becomes a Neang at once. Promotion is mandatory.

ការរាប់Counting

KhmerEnglishMeaning
ការរាប់countingThe weaker side counts; if the count reaches its limit before checkmate, the game is drawn. No direct equivalent in international chess (which has the fifty-move rule instead).
កិត្តិយសក្ដារBoard's HonorCounting from 1 to 64 by a side with three pieces or fewer (and fewer than the opponent).
កិត្តិយសកូនអុកPiece's HonorCounting by a side with a bare Sdach, from the total number of pieces on the board, up to a limit set by the stronger side's force.

The full explanation, with the table of limits and examples, is on the counting rules page.

ពាក្យទូទៅTerms from chess in general

  • Fork: one move that attacks two pieces at once. See the Ses fork in the strategy guide.
  • Pin: an attack on a piece that cannot move without exposing a more valuable one behind it.
  • Back rank: the first rank of a side, where its pieces start. A back-rank mate is checkmate on that rank (see checkmate patterns).
  • Discovered check: a check made by moving a piece out of the line of another piece.
  • Smothered mate: checkmate where the Sdach is boxed in by its own pieces.
  • Zugzwang: a position where the side to move would rather pass, because every move makes things worse.

Notation in this guide is simple: a piece's name, then the square it leaves, then "–" for a move or "×" for a capture, then the square it reaches, so "Tuuk g1–g8" is a Tuuk moving from g1 to g8.
